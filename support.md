# Bàrdachd — Support

Bàrdachd is a writing app for poets: live scansion, a rhyme finder, and
guidance for twelve fixed forms — on your iPhone, iPad or Mac, with nothing
sent anywhere.

## Getting help

Email **88_intake.troupes@icloud.com**. Say which device you are on and what
you were doing, and include a screenshot if there is anything to see.

## Common questions

**Where are my poems kept?**
On your device, and in your own iCloud account so your devices share one set.
There is no Bàrdachd account and no Bàrdachd server, and nobody but you can
see any of it. See the [privacy policy](privacy.md).

**How do I save a poem?**
There is no save button. Every change is written back automatically about half
a second after you stop typing, and again the instant you leave the poem or the
app goes to the background. A quiet "Saved" in the corner of the editor
confirms it.

**Why does the syllable count look wrong to me?**
Often you will be right, and there are three reasons why.

The dictionary is American, so it says four syllables for *secretary* where
most of Britain says three. Poetry elides — *heaven* and *flower* are two
syllables in any dictionary and one in half the lines ever written, and which
is right depends on the line rather than the word. And Gaelic words, Scots
words, place names and coinages are not in the dictionary at all; those are
estimated from their spelling and marked with a dotted underline so you know
the app is guessing.

So the dictionary gets a vote, not a veto. Tap any word in the analysis panel,
set how many syllables it has and which of them carry a beat, and the whole app
reads it that way from then on. The panel also lists the words it had to guess
at, so you can correct them in one pass rather than hunting for them.

**Why does it show beats *and* syllables? Aren't they the same?**
No, and that is the most useful thing the app tells you. They line up two to
one only in duple metres like the iamb. A limerick line is three beats and
eight or nine syllables, because anapaests run da-da-DUM. Anyone told simply to
"write ten syllables" for an anapaestic line has been misled, so both numbers
are always shown.

**It says my sonnet is only 76% on form.**
Form-matching scores against strict metre on purpose. A deliberate, skilful
variation — the trochaic first foot that opens Sonnet 18, say — reads here as a
departure. That is intended. The panel shows you *where* a line leaves the
template; whether that is a mistake or a good variation is your judgement, not
the app's.

**How do I find rhymes?**
The rhyme finder opens on the last word of the line you're writing, and lists
perfect and near rhymes ranked by how common each word is, so obscure surnames
and initialisms don't crowd out the useful ones. Some words have no rhyme at
all — *orange*, *month*, *silver*. Look at the near rhymes, or move the word off
the end of the line.

**The app looks empty when I open it.**
Check the sidebar. Your poems are at the top, then Forms, Rhyme and Exercises,
then the reference lists. If you have started a poem in free verse there is no
guidance to show, because free verse has none — set a form from the header of
the guidance box and it fills in.

**How do I delete a poem?**
Right-click it in the sidebar or in All poems, or swipe it. You can also delete
the poem you are in from the **⋯** menu. Anything with words in it asks first.

**Where are the rest of my poems?**
The sidebar keeps the six you touched most recently. **All poems** below them
has the lot, with search, sorting and grouping by date.

**How do I get a poem out of the app?**
**⋯** → Share as text, or Share as PDF. Both go through the normal share sheet,
so they can go anywhere you can send a file.

**Can I use it on my Mac as well as my phone?**
Yes — it runs on the Mac through Catalyst, using the same wide two-column
layout as the iPad. It's the same app, not a separate one.

**Will my poems be on all my devices?**
Yes. Start a poem on your phone and it is on the Mac; keep writing on the iPad
and the changes are on both. It syncs through your own iCloud account, not
through any server of mine — see the [privacy policy](privacy.md).

There is nothing to switch on: if you are signed in to iCloud on the device, it
is already happening. If you are not signed in, or you turn iCloud Drive off
for Bàrdachd in your device's Settings, the app carries on and keeps that
device's poems to itself.

**What dictionary does it use for pronunciation and rhymes?**
The CMU Pronouncing Dictionary (Carnegie Mellon University), with word
frequencies from the open-source wordfreq project. Both are credited in the
app's About screen, which is where their licences require it.

## What Bàrdachd does not do

No accounts, no subscription, no advertising, no analytics. Apart from iCloud
sync it makes no network requests at all — the whole pronunciation dictionary
is inside the app, so scansion and rhymes work with no signal.
